Alcohol Withdrawal Syndrome in Adult Traumatic Brain Injury with Alcohol Use Disorder

National Inpatient Sample, 2017–2022

Author
Affiliation

Muhammad Saad

Dow University of Health Sciences

Key Findings

  • The cohort represented 249,570 hospitalizations nationally; 58,270 (23.3%) had coded alcohol withdrawal syndrome (AWS).
  • AWS was documented in 23.3% of hospitalizations across 2017–2022.
  • AWS was associated with lower adjusted odds of in-hospital death (adjusted odds ratio [aOR], 0.60 (0.53–0.67)).
  • AWS was associated with higher adjusted odds of invasive ventilation (1.42 (1.34–1.50)), pneumonia or aspiration (2.32 (2.17–2.49)), institutional discharge among survivors (1.74 (1.65–1.84)), and discharge against medical advice among survivors (1.49 (1.34–1.66)).
  • Hospitalizations with AWS had longer stays (11.43 days vs 6.74 days) and higher costs ($42,702 vs $29,159).

Methods

Data source and ethics

The Healthcare Cost and Utilization Project (HCUP) National Inpatient Sample (NIS), developed by the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality (AHRQ), is an all-payer database built from a stratified sample of approximately 20% of discharges from U.S. community hospitals and weighted to national estimates. We analyzed 2017–2022, the ICD-10-CM/PCS era. The unit was a hospitalization, not a person. The data are de-identified and publicly available under the HCUP Data Use Agreement; this secondary analysis did not require institutional review board review or informed consent. Small cells are suppressed under HCUP disclosure rules. This study follows the RECORD (Reporting of studies Conducted using Observational Routinely-collected Data) extension of STROBE.

Study population

We selected hospitalizations in this order: age 18 years or older; acute TBI in any of 40 diagnosis fields, defined by an S06 code with seventh character A and seven characters total; active alcohol use disorder (AUD), defined by F10.1 or F10.2 codes after excluding remission-only F10.11/F10.21; and known in-hospital mortality status (DIED 0 or 1). The unit remained a hospitalization throughout. The cohort selection appears in Figure 1; Table S1 lists the ICD-10-CM/PCS definitions.

Exposure

AWS was defined by any of F10.230, F10.231, F10.232, F10.239, F10.130, F10.131, F10.132, or F10.139 in any diagnosis field. Withdrawal delirium codes (F10.231 and F10.131) were included. F10.13x codes were counted when present; the code audit verifies that they do not appear before October 2020.

Outcomes

In-hospital death was DIED. Invasive mechanical ventilation (IMV) was identified with ICD-10-PCS 5A1935Z, 5A1945Z, or 5A1955Z. Pneumonia included diagnosis prefixes J12–J18; aspiration pneumonitis was J69.0, and the combined outcome was either. Institutional discharge was DISPUNIFORM 2 or 5; discharge against medical advice (AMA) was DISPUNIFORM 7. Both disposition outcomes were analyzed among survivors with known disposition (DISPUNIFORM 1, 2, 5, 6, or 7); home health (6) was not institutional. LOS was the recorded length of stay in days. Hospital cost was TOTCHG multiplied by CCR_NIS and adjusted to 2022 U.S. dollars using the CPI-U medical-care index implemented in easyNIS. Cost was unavailable when TOTCHG or CCR_NIS was missing or CCR_NIS was non-positive; zero or negative cost values were excluded from the cost model.

Covariates

The adjustment set was age per 10 years, sex, race/ethnicity (RACE), primary expected payer (PAY1), ZIP-code income quartile (ZIPINC_QRTL), discharge year, hospital census region, bed size, location/teaching status, TBI subtype, skull fracture, extracranial injury, TBI as the principal diagnosis, chronic Elixhauser burden, liver disease, and other substance use disorder (SUD). The mutually exclusive TBI hierarchy assigned the most severe observed qualifying subtype: diffuse injury/brain compression (S06.2 or S06.A), epidural hemorrhage (S06.4), subdural hemorrhage (S06.5), contusion/focal injury/subarachnoid hemorrhage/edema (S06.1, S06.3, or S06.6), then concussion/other/unspecified (S06.0, S06.8, or S06.9). Skull fracture used S02.0, S02.1, or S02.91 with seventh character A or B; extracranial injury used S10–S99 with seventh character A, B, or C. Other SUD used F11–F16, F18, or F19, excluding remission-only codes. Liver disease used K70–K77.

The chronic comorbidity count was based on AHRQ Elixhauser Refined ICD-10 conditions scored by easyNIS/medicalcoder, grouped as 0, 1, 2, or 3 or more. The 29 conditions retained are listed in protocol/decisions.md; alcohol, liver disease, and other SUD were excluded because they were cohort features or separately adjusted, while acute trauma-related, neurologic, cerebrovascular, psychosis, blood-loss anemia, coagulopathy, fluid/electrolyte, and weight-loss conditions were excluded because NIS has no present-on-admission indicator to distinguish pre-existing illness from acute sequelae. Ventilation, pneumonia, neurosurgery, severity scores, and LOS were not adjustment variables because they may follow AWS or reflect the hospitalization course; the analysis did not measure neurosurgery.

Missing data

Adjusted models used complete cases. Table 2 gives each model’s weighted analytic N and percentage of outcome-eligible weighted discharges retained; Table S3 shows weighted missingness by AWS group for each adjustment variable and outcome. Table 1 displays observed missing categories as “Unknown” for payer, income, race, and other categorical variables with missing values; those records are excluded from adjusted models. Continuous age and sex are not recoded as Unknown.

Statistical analysis

The NIS survey design used hospital clusters (HOSP_NIS), strata (NIS_STRATUM), and discharge weights (DISCWT); cohort analyses were domain analyses on the full survey design. Lonely primary sampling units were handled with the survey option survey.lonely.psu = "adjust" (and survey.adjust.domain.lonely = TRUE). Table 1 group comparisons used the gtsummary::add_p() survey defaults: Rao–Scott chi-square tests for categorical variables and design-based Wilcoxon rank-sum tests for continuous variables. Binary outcomes used survey-weighted quasibinomial logistic regression and are reported as adjusted odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals. The model code also computes standardized probabilities by setting AWS to each level for the complete-case records, averaging predictions with normalized survey weights, and using a delta-method variance with t-based confidence limits from survey design degrees of freedom; Table 2 reports binary ORs and does not display those probability margins. LOS used a quasipoisson log-link model; cost used a Gamma log-link model among positive costs. Continuous outcomes are summarized by median (IQR), with adjusted differences in mean outcomes from predictive margins. Table S4 reports annual weighted prevalence and a survey logistic trend test with year as a continuous term; the vertical marker denotes October 2020, when the additional F10.13x codes became valid. Sensitivity analyses restricted the cohort to LOS ≥2 days to assess short-stay coding opportunity, required TBI as the principal diagnosis to assess diagnosis position, and excluded transferred-in discharges to assess transfer-related case mix. Tests were two-sided with α=0.05 and no multiplicity adjustment. Analyses used R 4.6.1, with the survey, gtsummary, and easyNIS packages. All counts are weighted national estimates and are shown as weighted n (weighted %); cells with unweighted counts ≤10 are suppressed. The analysis included 49,914 sampled hospitalizations, representing 249,570 hospitalizations nationally.

In-Hospital Outcomes

The table presents crude comparisons and adjusted associations for the prespecified outcomes. Binary outcomes are weighted counts and percentages; LOS and cost are weighted medians (IQR). The model estimates compare odds for binary outcomes and mean differences for continuous outcomes between hospitalizations with AWS and those without AWS.

Survey-weighted national estimates. Binary outcomes are shown as weighted n (%) with odds ratios from survey-weighted logistic regression. Length of stay and cost are right-skewed, so they are summarized as median (IQR). Differences are estimated with survey-weighted generalized linear models with a log link (Poisson for length of stay, gamma for cost). These models handle skewed data without transforming the outcome, and the results are reported as differences in adjusted mean values (predictive margins). Adjusted models include all covariates listed in Methods. Costs are inflation-adjusted to 2022 US dollars. Institutional discharge and discharge against medical advice are among survivors.

In the adjusted models, the aOR was 0.60 (0.53–0.67) for death, 1.42 (1.34–1.50) for IMV, 2.32 (2.17–2.49) for pneumonia/aspiration, 1.74 (1.65–1.84) for institutional discharge, and 1.49 (1.34–1.66) for AMA. Adjusted mean differences were +4.69 days (+4.38 to +5.00) days for LOS and +$13,543 (+$12,456 to +$14,630) for cost.

The mortality association remained inverse across the three prespecified sensitivity analyses: LOS ≥2 days 0.70 (0.62–0.80), TBI principal diagnosis 0.58 (0.51–0.66), and excluding transferred-in hospitalizations 0.59 (0.52–0.68).

Limitations

This analysis uses administrative discharge records, so diagnoses may be missing or inconsistently coded. The NIS does not provide diagnosis timing or Present on Admission indicators; we cannot determine whether AWS or other outcomes began before or during the hospitalization. The lower mortality association may reflect survivor and coding bias: patients who die early or have short stays may have less opportunity for AWS to be recognized and coded. The LOS ≥2 days sensitivity analysis addresses part of this concern but cannot remove it. The database also lacks detailed clinical severity measures, linked records across hospitals, and inpatient medication data. Findings should therefore be interpreted as associations among hospitalizations, not treatment effects or patient-level risks.
